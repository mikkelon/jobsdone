Scratchpad

A post-it block, on its own page. gn switches the whole window to Notes and esc back, so a note gets real width; the day and backlog are one key away, not squeezed beside it. Text only.

  1. The pane header names Notes and the key that archives one; the status line counts them and says how to get back. Commands and help still work here; / filters the notes instead of searching the tasks.
  2. Notes are a list, newest first: first line and age. No titles, no folders. Create, open, archive, throw away; neither archiving nor deleting asks, and both undo. Nothing expires on its own.
  3. An open note takes the wide pane and shows when it was made, so a stale note is easy to spot and bin.
  4. The note is a plain multi-line text area with a cursor. No formatting, no toolbar. Every key types; esc returns focus to the list, where a, x and g work as keys.
Why a page and not a drawerA drawer beside the day left a note about 40 columns wide, which is a strip, not a sheet. A page gives it 75 columns in the floating window and everything in a tile. The cost is one keypress to see the day again.
  1. tab puts the Archive in the list's place, and back. A on a note archives it, and on the Archive brings it back; the cursor lands on the row that took its place. Archived notes are dated by when they were archived, most recent first, and open and take typing like any other.
  2. An archived note's header says when it was made and when it was archived.
  3. / opens a filter at the top of whichever list is showing. Letters type into it and the list narrows as they do, best match first: the letters of every word in order, anywhere in the body. ↑/↓ move, ⏎ opens, tab hands the keyboard to the list with the filter still applied, and esc clears it. The filter stays while a note is opened from it, and goes with tab to the other list.
Narrow layoutNotes and the Archive are the last two stops of tab (screen 04); the list and the open note stack in that tab.
